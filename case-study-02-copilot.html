The Details

Swipe

The Problem

Microsoft Priva is a suite of five privacy products — subject rights requests, consent management, privacy risk management, assessments, and tracker scanning — that together cover an organisation's privacy compliance workflow. Privacy officers working across that suite faced a consistent challenge: the tools existed, but navigating across five distinct products to answer a single compliance question was slow and cognitively demanding.

Understanding consent requirements across jurisdictions, initiating a data subject request, and reviewing risk patterns required expert knowledge of where everything lived. With generative AI maturing rapidly inside Microsoft, the question became: what does it look like when an AI assistant orchestrates privacy operations — not just answers questions?

Research

An AI-forward privacy solution was unheard of at the time — there was no reference implementation to borrow from, so the interaction model had to be derived from scratch and tested continuously. I paid close attention to that core interaction model: how the AI surfaces suggestions, how users keep control over AI-generated content, and how the assistant moves between conversation and native product UI.

We ran RITE studies — rapid iterative testing and evaluation, revising the prototype between sessions rather than waiting for a full round to finish — alongside regular internal UX and product team reviews. Each pass narrowed which patterns privacy officers actually trusted with a real compliance decision.

The board shows the volume that took: dated, dense, iterated on daily. Most of what's on it didn't ship — that discarded work is how the final patterns got settled.

Insight & Decisions

Underlying every pattern were two constraints: AI suggestions needed to be attributable, editable, and interruptible, or privacy officers wouldn't trust the system with real compliance decisions — and don't recreate UI inside Copilot itself, leverage the product's existing UI rather than making users re-learn flows they already know.

Smart wizard automation. Rather than filling 11+ fields manually, the user got a wizard pre-populated with Copilot's suggestions, each one editable and clearly labeled as a suggestion rather than a locked answer.

Magic Spotlight. The vision was to let a user click into any artifact they had a question about — not just a chart — and ask Copilot directly.

Outcome

A task that previously took manual research and form-filling was reduced to a review-and-confirm. The Magic Spotlight vision shipped largely as designed — once a user's home populates with their history, a Copilot icon surfaces a contextual answer without leaving the page.

One direction we explored further, an inline expansion that turned a card into a small embedded chat panel, was ultimately not shipped: accessibility and technical feasibility concerns ruled it out in favor of the simpler icon-triggered pattern.

Impact

The prototype was demoed live at the IAPP Global Summit on April 3, 2024. Response from privacy professionals was strongly positive — generative AI applied specifically to privacy compliance was still uncommon to see live at that point, and the demo landed as a credible, production-ready vision rather than a research concept.

It became an internal blueprint: interaction patterns from this project — particularly the trust model and the smart wizard flow — closely resemble what shipped later in Microsoft's broader Security Copilot roadmap.
